Invoice vs. estimate: what’s the difference?

An estimate explains proposed work and pricing. An invoice requests payment for the amount being billed.

By Wolfpack Labs · 4 min read
In this guideAn estimate helps agree on the workAn invoice tells the customer what to payKeep the two documents connectedA simple workflow to follow

An estimate helps agree on the work

An estimate sets out the proposed scope, expected quantities, pricing, and assumptions. It gives the customer something concrete to review before the job begins.

Spell out what is included, what is excluded, and how you will handle changes. If the price depends on conditions you have not inspected, explain that rather than presenting uncertainty as a fixed promise.

An invoice tells the customer what to pay

An invoice identifies the work being billed, any adjustments or deposits, the balance due, and payment instructions. You might invoice after completion, at an agreed milestone, or for an agreed deposit.

DocumentMain purposeUseful details
EstimateReview proposed work and pricingScope, assumptions, validity period, approval process
InvoiceRequest paymentInvoice number, amount due, due date, payment method

Keep the two documents connected

Reference the estimate on the invoice. If the work changes, document the change and the customer’s approval before treating the new price as agreed.

For example, an estimate may cover painting one room. If the customer adds a hallway, the final invoice should make the added work visible. Do not quietly replace the original scope with an unexplained total.

A simple workflow to follow

  1. Write down the scope and proposed price.
  2. Ask the customer to review and approve it.
  3. Record any agreed changes.
  4. Create an invoice that reflects the agreed billing stage.
  5. Record payment and any remaining balance.

The legal effect of an estimate or quote depends on its wording and the agreement. For this everyday workflow, the useful distinction is proposed pricing versus a request for payment.
