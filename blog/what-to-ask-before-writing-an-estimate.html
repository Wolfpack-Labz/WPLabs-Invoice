What to ask a customer before you write an estimate

Collect the scope, site details, timing and billing information that make an estimate easier to prepare and understand.

By Wolfpack Labs · October 5, 2026 · 3 min read
In this guideStart with the result the customer wantsSeparate confirmed details from assumptionsAsk about access and timingIdentify the right contact and billing detailsSend a short recap before pricing

Start with the result the customer wants

“I need the room painted” is a useful opening, but it is not enough information to price the job. Is the customer asking for walls only, or ceilings, trim and doors too? Is the room empty? Are repairs needed before the first coat?

Ask the customer to describe what a finished job should look like. Then turn that description into specific work you can estimate. A few minutes of clarification can reveal a different project from the one you first pictured.

Use plain questions: What needs to change? Which areas are included? What would make the result acceptable? If you need to inspect or measure before giving a firm price, say that early.

Separate confirmed details from assumptions

Record the job address, measurements, materials and any known condition that affects the work. A photo may help explain the request, but it cannot confirm every dimension or hidden issue. Label anything you have not checked.

  • Confirmed: the customer wants two rooms painted, including trim.
  • To verify: room dimensions, wall repairs and the condition of the existing finish.
  • Assumption: the customer will move furniture before the scheduled start.

Keep these distinctions in your notes and carry relevant assumptions into the estimate. If your price depends on a site visit or a particular material choice, the customer should be able to see that.

Ask about access and timing

Find out when you can enter the property, where equipment can be unloaded and whether other work will be happening at the same time. Stairs, limited parking, occupied rooms and restricted hours may change the time required.

Ask whether the requested finish date is flexible or tied to another event. “Before the house is listed” is different from “sometime this month.” Record the requested timing without promising a slot you have not checked.

Include setup, cleanup and the return visits you expect. Use the Contractor Pricing Calculator to bring labor, materials, other job costs and allocated overhead together. Count each cost once.

Identify the right contact and billing details

The person showing you the work may not be the person approving the price or paying the invoice. Ask who should receive the estimate, who will approve changes and what billing name or job reference is needed.

Confirm the customer's preferred contact method and the address for documents. Read back an unfamiliar email address before sending. If a business customer needs a purchase order or vendor setup, find out before the job is ready to invoice.

Collect information needed for the work and billing. An intake sheet is not a place for payment card numbers, account passwords or other unnecessary sensitive details. Keep completed forms private.

Send a short recap before pricing

Finish the conversation with a concise recap: the work requested, what is included, what still needs checking and the next step. This gives the customer a chance to correct a misunderstanding while it is easy to change.

Example recap

“I will estimate painting the two bedrooms, including trim. I still need to confirm measurements and wall repairs. You will move the furniture before work starts. After the site visit, I will send the scope and price for review.”

Once the details are ready, use the Estimate Worksheet. Keep the intake record alongside the estimate so you can trace where the scope and assumptions came from.
