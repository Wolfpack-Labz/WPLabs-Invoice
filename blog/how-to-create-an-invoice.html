How to create an invoice for a small business

A clear invoice makes it easier for your customer to understand the work, the total, and how to pay.

By Wolfpack Labs · 6 min read
In this guideStart with the essentialsDescribe the work so it is easy to recognizeCheck the arithmeticMake paying the invoice straightforwardBefore you press send

Start with the essentials

You do not need elaborate design software to create an invoice. Start with a document that clearly answers three questions: who is billing, what is being billed, and when payment is due.

  • Your business name and contact information.
  • The customer’s name and billing contact.
  • A unique invoice number and the date issued.
  • An itemized description of the work or products.
  • Quantities, rates, and line totals.
  • The amount due, due date, and payment instructions.

Describe the work so it is easy to recognize

“Services: $500” leaves room for questions. “Living-room painting: 10 hours × $50” gives the customer a clear connection between the work and the charge. Add a job address or project reference when it helps distinguish one job from another.

For a fixed-price project, use the agreed project description and price. Make extra work a separate line item and keep the customer’s approval with the job records.

Check the arithmetic

Multiply each quantity by its rate, add the line totals, and show adjustments separately. Here is a simple example without tax:

ItemAmount
Labor: 10 hours × $50$500
Materials$120
Subtotal$620
Deposit already paid−$100
Balance due$520

Use the tax treatment that applies to your business and transaction. Do not add tax simply because a template has a tax field.

Make paying the invoice straightforward

Use an exact due date rather than leaving the customer to work it out. Include the payment method or secure payment link you actually accept. Double-check the destination before sending.

Send the invoice to the person responsible for paying, save a copy, and record the date sent. A consistent routine matters more than decorative details.

Before you press send

Check the customer name, agreed scope, invoice number, total, deposits, and due date. Read the payment instructions as if you were seeing them for the first time. Could the customer pay without sending you another message?
