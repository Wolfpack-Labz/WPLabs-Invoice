Outlook Not Sending or Receiving Emails? Troubleshoot Sync and Connection Problems

Learn how to diagnose whether Outlook, your account, or the mail service is at fault—then fix stuck messages, offline mode, and profile problems.

Start here: identify the cause

When Outlook stops sending or receiving mail, repeatedly pressing Send does not tell you what is broken. You need to separate mail-service problems from Outlook application problems. The steps below cover both new Outlook and classic Outlook for Windows, with differences called out clearly.

Protect unsynced mail

Confirm that webmail works and preserve any local-only Outlook data before changing profiles or removing accounts.

First: determine whether the mailbox works in a browser

  1. Open your email provider's webmail service in a browser—for Microsoft 365 work accounts, typically Outlook on the web through your organization's approved sign-in link.
  2. Send a short test email to a separate address you control, then reply to it.
  3. If webmail also cannot send or receive, check for service alerts or account restrictions. On company accounts, ask IT to check Microsoft 365 service health, mailbox limits, and sign-in status.
  4. If webmail works normally but the desktop app does not, the issue is more likely local to Outlook, its cached session, or its profile.

Important: Do not delete your Outlook account, PST files, or locally stored messages before confirming that unsynced mail and contacts are backed up.

Step 1: Confirm internet access and the correct account

Open two unrelated websites. Then verify the mailbox address shown in Outlook is the one receiving the missing messages. If your password recently changed, follow Outlook's sign-in prompt to authenticate again; don't repeatedly enter credentials into unfamiliar pop-ups.

For work accounts, a VPN, security policy, or account lockout can affect connectivity. Use your employer's approved access procedures.

Step 2: Force a manual sync—the option depends on your version

New Outlook for Windows:

  1. Open Mail.
  2. Select View → Sync on the ribbon, or press F9.
  3. Wait for the sync status message to finish.

Classic Outlook for Windows:

  1. Select the Send/Receive tab.
  2. Choose Send/Receive All Folders, or press F9.
  3. Check the status bar for Disconnected, Trying to connect, or Working Offline.

Third-party IMAP, POP, and other providers may support different manual sync behavior. Do not assume that a Sync button proves the server received a message.

Step 3: Turn off Work Offline in classic Outlook

In classic Outlook, go to Send/Receive → Work Offline. If it is selected, click it to return online. Then run Send/Receive All Folders. This control does not appear in the same place in new Outlook.

If Outlook continues showing “Disconnected,” check network/VPN access and account authentication before modifying the profile.

Step 4: Inspect messages stuck in the Outbox

  1. Open Outbox and locate any unsent message.
  2. Check for oversized attachments, invalid recipient addresses, or an expired draft session.
  3. Save any important message text or attachments before changing it.
  4. Replace a very large attachment with a secure file-sharing link if your mail provider supports it.
  5. Send a fresh, small test message and verify that it appears in Sent Items and is received at the destination.

A message appearing in Sent Items is useful evidence, but final delivery can still be blocked by recipient filters or server-side rules.

Step 5: Check folders, filters, and mailbox limits

If mail sends but new messages seem missing, search All folders, Junk, Other/Focused, Archive, and Deleted Items. Review any mailbox rules or filters that automatically move messages. For work or school mailboxes, have your administrator verify storage quotas and service alerts.

If only one contact's mail is missing, ask them to confirm the delivery address and whether they received a bounce-back message.

Step 6: Repair the account profile in classic Outlook

This method is for classic Outlook, and the Repair option is not available in every configuration.

  1. Choose File → Account Settings → Account Settings.
  2. On the Email tab, select the affected account.
  3. If available, choose Repair and follow the wizard.
  4. Restart Outlook and test mail flow again.

If Repair is unavailable or unsuccessful, ask IT to help create a new Outlook profile, especially with Exchange, shared mailboxes, or accounts holding local-only archives. Avoid removing a profile until you have confirmed what data is stored only on the computer.

Step 7: Investigate a conflicting add-in in classic Outlook

Close Outlook. Press Win + R and run:

outlook.exe /safe

Safe mode starts classic Outlook with add-ins disabled. If sending and receiving works there, reopen Outlook normally, review File → Options → Add-ins, and disable suspected add-ins individually to identify the cause. This is a diagnostic step for classic, not new Outlook.

How to verify the fix

Send and receive a test email, check another device, confirm the Outlook status is online, and repeat after restarting the app. If the browser works but desktop Outlook still fails, share the exact error message, account type, app version, and test results with support.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Outlook work on my phone but not on my laptop?

The mobile app and desktop app have separate cached credentials and settings. This often points to a desktop client or network issue, though it does not prove one.

Will creating a new Outlook profile delete my emails?

Server-stored mail may resync, but local PST archives and unsynced items might not. Verify backups before any profile removal.

Is the Outlook Sync button the same as Send/Receive?

They perform similar manual-refresh tasks in different Outlook versions, but the available options vary by account type.
