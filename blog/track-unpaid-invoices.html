How to track unpaid invoices without losing the thread

An unpaid invoice is easier to manage when you can see its due date, balance, and last customer contact in one place.

By Wolfpack Labs · 4 min read
In this guideKeep one reliable listUse a few clear statusesMake follow-up a weekly habitRecord partial payments carefully

Keep one reliable list

Start with a notebook, spreadsheet, or invoice app you will consistently use. Keep the invoice number, customer, issue date, due date, original total, amount paid, and remaining balance together.

Separate draft invoices from sent invoices. A draft that never reached the customer should not look like a customer who is late paying.

Use a few clear statuses

  • Sent: delivered to the customer and awaiting payment.
  • Partially paid: some payment recorded, with a balance remaining.
  • Overdue: past the agreed due date with an unpaid balance.
  • Paid: payment received and matched to the invoice.

Keep disputed invoices identifiable so they receive a conversation about the issue, rather than another routine reminder.

Make follow-up a weekly habit

Choose a regular day to review outstanding invoices. Check that the customer received the invoice and that the payment instructions still work. Record when you last followed up and what the customer said.

A short, neutral reminder is usually more useful than an accusatory message. Include the invoice number, balance, due date, and a copy or link so the customer does not have to search for it.

Record partial payments carefully

If an invoice was $800 and the customer paid $300, the remaining balance is $500. Preserve the original invoice amount and record the payment separately so you can see the history.

Reconcile your records with actual payments received. A customer saying “paid” is a prompt to check the transaction and match it to the right invoice.
