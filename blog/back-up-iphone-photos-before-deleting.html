How to back up iPhone photos before deleting them

Before clearing a photo library, make sure the pictures you want to keep exist somewhere you understand and can actually open. A visible cloud thumbnail is only part of that check.

Quick answer. Export a separate copy of the photos and videos you want to keep, open files from that destination, and confirm the copy is complete before deleting anything. With iCloud Photos enabled, deleting a photo in the synced library also deletes it from iCloud and other devices using that library.

Decide whether you need phone space or fewer photos

If you want to keep your library but use less space on the phone, start by reviewing iCloud Photos’ storage-optimization option. Apple explains that Optimize Storage can keep smaller device copies while full-resolution originals remain in iCloud. It is a different action from deleting a photo.

If you want to remove images from the library itself, preserve any you still want in a separate archive first. Seeing the same image on two synced devices does not establish that either copy is independent of a deletion.

Our cloud sync versus backup guide explains that distinction. For other ways to make room, check the storage breakdown described in our phone-storage guide before choosing your photos as the first cleanup target.

Choose a destination and a small test batch

Write down the copy you intend to make: which date range, where it will go, and whether you want unmodified originals, edited versions, or both. For example: “Family photos from January through June, exported to my external drive, checked on my laptop.”

Use a clearly named folder, such as Photo-Archive-2026-Jan-Jun. Check that the destination has enough room and that you can find it again after closing the export screen.

Begin with a small batch containing an older photo, a recent photo, a video, and any special format you care about. This helps you discover a format or destination problem before you attempt the entire library. Menu names can vary by software version; use the official guides linked below for your device.

Export directly from an iPhone

Apple’s archiving guide describes copying photos to connected external storage. Connect a supported storage device, select the items in Photos, and use the share menu’s unmodified-original export option. Choose the intended external destination under Locations and finish saving.

If originals still need to download, allow time and enough device space for that process. Apple’s guide includes the setting for keeping originals on the device. Do not assume that opening a thumbnail has completed a full-library export.

After saving, inspect the folder through Files or on a computer. Make sure you are opening the exported files, rather than going back into the Photos library and viewing the synced originals again.

Use a Mac or a browser for another export route

On a Mac using iCloud Photos, Apple’s download instructions include Photos → Settings → iCloud → Download Originals to this Mac. Downloading originals into a synced library is only one step; export the files you want into your chosen archive location.

For the export itself, Apple’s Mac Photos guide describes selecting photos, choosing File → Export, and selecting export settings and a destination. Choose the version you actually want to preserve.

On a Mac or PC, you can also sign in at iCloud Photos on the web, select items, and use its download controls. Apple documents options including unmodified originals and compatible formats, and a selection limit of 1,000 items at a time. For a larger library, use batches you can reconcile.

If the download arrives as an archive, extract it and check the files inside. A downloaded filename by itself does not tell you whether all the pictures and videos you expected are present.

Verify the copy before deciding what to delete

  • Open several exported photos directly from the destination, including older and newer items.
  • Play exported videos and confirm the parts you care about are present.
  • Check whether an export preserved the original or the edited appearance you intended.
  • Compare the selected date range and batches with what actually arrived.
  • Keep a note of the destination and export date.

Counts can be a useful check, but they are not the whole check. An export may include multiple files for one item or extra companion files. If something is unclear, reconcile a small sample rather than assuming matching totals prove a complete archive.

For irreplaceable material, consider a second independently stored copy. That is a practical recovery choice: a drive can be misplaced or fail, and a folder can be deleted. Choose a routine you can repeat and verify.

Clean up only after you understand the consequences

Apple states that iCloud Photos changes—including deletions—propagate across devices using the library. It also notes that photos already using iCloud Photos are not duplicated in the iCloud device backup. Do not count that device backup as a second photo archive.

If you decide to delete, work in a small batch and read the confirmation. Do not treat Recently Deleted as your long-term archive or clear it simply because a storage screen suggests another cleanup opportunity.

Continue exporting new photos on a schedule that fits how often you take them. Label the next batch so you know where the previous one ended. The useful result is a photo library you can manage and an archive you have checked, rather than a one-time space saving you cannot reverse.
