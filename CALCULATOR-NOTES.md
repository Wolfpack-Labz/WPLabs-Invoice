# Calculator methods

All monetary results use US dollars and are displayed to two decimal places. Inputs and calculations remain in the visitor's page; no values are saved or submitted. Example amounts are provided as a starting point. Clearing or invalidating a required input clears the previous result. Reset returns to the examples.

## Contractor Pricing

Labor cost is hours multiplied by the labor cost per hour. Materials, other job costs and allocated overhead are added once. Price before sales tax is the total cost divided by one minus the target margin expressed as a decimal. Profit is selling price less included costs. At least one cost must be positive; the margin must be between 0% and 99.99%.

## Profit Margin

Profit equals revenue less included costs. Margin is profit divided by revenue. Markup is profit divided by cost. Selling price must be positive, and costs may be zero. A loss is negative. Zero cost leaves markup undefined instead of showing infinity. The cost scope determines which margin the result describes.

## Late Fees

Monthly fees use a 30-day month. Annual fees use a fixed 365-day year. Daily fees multiply by each day late. The one-time method applies a percentage once when days late is positive. Zero days produces a zero fee in every method. No fees compound. The calculated fee is rounded to cents before it is added to the outstanding balance. Days late must be a non-negative whole number and exclude any agreed grace period. The calculator does not determine whether a fee is permitted.

## Hourly Rate

Annual billable hours equal working weeks multiplied by billable hours per week. Break-even rate is annual owner pay plus business overhead divided by annual billable hours. Target rate is the break-even rate divided by one minus the target business profit margin. Owner pay is before personal taxes. Business profit is the additional amount retained after owner pay and entered overhead. Separately charged materials, subcontractors and other job costs are excluded unless entered as business expenses. Working weeks must be positive and no more than 52; weekly billable hours must be positive and no more than 168.

## Markup vs Margin

With a markup percentage, price equals cost multiplied by one plus that percentage as a decimal. With a target margin, price equals cost divided by one minus that decimal. The result shows price, profit and both percentage measures. Cost must be positive. Markup may be 100% or higher; margin must be below 100%.

## Invoice Due Date

Add the selected calendar-day term to the invoice date, counting the invoice date as day zero. Net 30 on January 1 is January 31. Custom terms support 0-3,650 whole days. The date starts at today in the visitor's local time zone. Calendar arithmetic and result formatting use date-only values in UTC so daylight-saving changes cannot shift the result. Dates support years 0001-9999.

Weekend handling is optional. A Saturday moves two days and a Sunday moves one day to Monday. The original calendar date is shown as well. Holidays are not considered.
